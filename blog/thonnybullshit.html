And there he was. Jonny. In the kitchen. Stoic as always. A heavy weight in the room, words unsaid; they didn't need to be. The atmosphere makes it clear. 9AM again, far too early for both of us, and he knows that. Jonny stands near the stovetop in a night gown. He’s overthinking again. So have I. We’re a month into this relationship. Dating my bandmate, my prior schoolmate, my everything. All or nothing, and I can’t seem to do anything right.

He’s cooking breakfast again, like every morning, oblivious to my presence. Going through the motions, stoic and uncomfortable. Bags under his eyes, like he hasn’t slept in days. Drained, and I am too. I don’t remember the last time he fell asleep in my arms. Sleep doesn’t come easy for either of us. The bedroom feels like a staring contest with the ceiling; I just want to see him happy, well rested. Maybe pull him out of his own head one in a while. Jonny never tells me what's upsetting him, what is eating him alive. He lets it swallow us both whole. Like I used to. Guilt? Pain? Trauma? Maybe I’ll never know.

Jonny is wearing a tight apron over his night gown, like he’s trying to hide the fact that he hasn’t changed out of it in days. Unshowered, drenched in dried sweat. His posture is awkward. The same awkwardness that drew me in when we first met. The spark. It all feels hollow now. Morning light filters through the blinds, catching in his black hair and the faint shine of it. It settles in the hollows beneath his eyes, weighing him down. His hands clutch the frying pan like it’s the only thing left in the world, gathering ingredients on autopilot, no reason to even have a pan in his hand in the first place.

And then, an odd scent. Meat. Bacon, and sausage. Jonny knows I’m a vegetarian, I have been my whole life. How could he have forgotten? I stare at him from the stairway. In his own world. Fascinating. The same world I wish I could break him out of sometimes, when it’s just us. When the world doesn’t feel so heavy. The meat sizzles against the pan with a slow, violent hiss. He flinches. The first emotion I’ve seen from him all week. Maybe he thinks he’s alone. Maybe he is better off without me. I don’t want to let go.

And then the sharp shattering of a plate. I flinch as well at the sound, Jonny goes into full blown panic, sweeping it up as if the motion will suppress whatever emotions it brought up. Probably trauma. I haven’t figured him out enough to know. We’ve been friends our whole lives. Long before this relationship. His apron highlights the uncomfortable arch in his back as he sweeps up the shattered plate, the tension in him is obvious. All I can do is watch, wishing I could take it all away.

I prop myself up against the stairwell, watching. It feels a lot better to watch, to not interact. It’s all shutdowns and pleading, and it’s driving me crazy. Jonny spaces out at the stovetop, almost burning the meat, the meat that shouldn’t be cooked in the first place, but then he snaps back. I wish I could be the one to snap him back into reality, but it seems like I’m always just a spectator. Maybe I always will be. Pathetic.

The familiar sounds of a knife at a chopping board. Chopping up vegetables that don’t go with the breakfast at all. Slice. The knife catches his index finger. On accident, or on purpose. I can’t tell. Blood slips from the cut, pooling on the cutting board. He remains expressionless. Unfazed. I cough a bit, getting his attention. Jonny’s eyes dart towards me, frantic, as he wraps his hand in a dishrag. What am I meant to say?

“Hey…” I say, awkwardly, playing with the inner seam of my sleeve. I must look like I’ve seen a ghost, or maybe my returned stoicism is finally working, though it never seems to. Jonny stares at me, his expression unreadable, his hand wrapped in a dishrag, dripping blood, and relaxed in his other one gently, with a slight curl of his fingers. His skin is pale, thinner than ever before, starved almost, but I’ll never tell him how much it bothers me.

I don’t know how long we stare at each other for. The silence consumes us, swallows us whole. The outside world feels as if it pauses. My eyes fixated on his, despite my difficulty with eye contact. Maybe if I stare at him long enough I’ll finally figure out what’s on his mind. Or maybe it's all futile, like every other day.

“You're making bacon and sausage.” The first thought in my head, and I blurt it out. Jonny’s hand tenses a little, forcing a little bit more blood out of his hand. Like he wants to bleed. His face is still stoic, but the slight hint of pain translates through his eyes, like he already knows he messed up.

“You know I’m vegetarian. That’s not something you just forget… right?” I should be making this about him. But I choose whatever conversation feels safe, whatever conversation will keep him from shutting down again, going back into himself. Jonny’s face is blank, his hand tenses a bit more as I start walking towards him.

“Take it easy… please?” I walk slowly, like I’m approaching a wounded animal. He might as well be one at this point. I gently slide my hands to his waist and grip, afraid he’ll vanish if I let go. His body goes stiff, like always. I don’t know how else to get it through to him. I know it’s wrong, but I cup his injured hand anyway, pressing carefully against the finger wrapped in the dishrag. “It doesn’t have to be so hard… Jonny”I say, Wincing internally at the honesty, but I don’t know what else to say. The stovetop is off, the bacon and sausage lukewarm now, slightly burnt. One of my hands is still around his waist, the other holding his wounded hand, like the world is going to fall through if I ever dare to let go.

A small “Yeah.” fills up the silence of the kitchen. Quietly. Detached almost. Automatic. Typical of him, I don’t remember the last time we actually had a meaningful conversation, if ever. It seems to all go in circles, a neverending loop that swallows us both.

“Please… I cant.. I can't do this…” My tone sounds desperate, pathetic almost. Maybe I’ll never break through. Maybe he’ll never truly be here, always stuck in his head. An instinct rushes through me. To protect. I grip his injured hand tighter, it finally stops bleeding. My hands are stained red, but I don’t care. As long as he is still breathing, I’ll be red. The blood will always circle down the drain, his pain won’t.

Another break of silence. “I know” Jonny mutters, small almost. Vulnerable. A small tremble in his voice, the second bit of emotion I have seen in him, like the lack of sleep is finally catching up with him.

Impulsively, I let out ”Jonny.. I love you…” I don’t know what else to say. My voice cracks, a high pitched squeak as I hold back tears. A million emotions rush through me at once, but I want to stay strong, for him. All for him.

It dawns on me. I’ve been too stuck inside of my head. Jonny was bleeding, his hand still in a dishrag. Too focused on myself, too focused on all my weaknesses to go get him the first aid kit. Too busy in my own head to put a bandaid on him. ”I.. I’m gonna go get… the first aid kit…” I hesitate. Should I add… I don’t know… ”J.. Just don't disappear on me… please?” I don’t even know what I’m saying at this point. Words rush through my head a thousand miles a second. Thoughts, scenarios, ideas. All meaningless despite my whole career being deep and impactful lyrics. No song could fix this type of strain, maybe it never will.

The walk to the bathroom feels far, like each step I take the hallway lengthens. The walls feel like they are taunting me, laughing at me. I can’t even take care of my own boyfriend, my bandmate, my everything. It’s all falling apart on me. My skin feels like it’s pulling apart, cell by cell. I choke it all down, hoping it’ll change something. I open the bathroom door with too much force, holding back tears, but I can’t cry. Not right now, or ever. Just.. stay strong.

I open the medicine cabinet, spacing out a bit, cursing myself when I snap back to. I forgot why the hell I was here in the first place until I see the blood still stained on my hands. I’m not being fast enough. I frantically grab the first aid kit, and then I rush back to the kitchen. Jonny is standing in front of the sink, the dishrag now in the sink, washing off his own blood from his hand. Like I’m not even here.

“N.. No.. Jonny.. Careful..” I say while gently cupping his wrist, resisting the urge to shake him until he finally understands. Until he snaps back. ”Please let me fix this” My voice is sharp, shaky. The plea burns in my throat worse than any lyric ever could, than any lyric should. I gently wash the blood off of his hand, it circles down the drain slowly.

Another gap of silence broken by ”You're so.. strong” The statement feels hollow on my lips. How is that meant to tell him how I really feel? The sleepless nights, the passion, the begging. God, even gestures don’t seem to work anymore, or touch.

I desperately add ”Jonny.. Please.. Can you talk?” Like it’ll work. Like any of this ever works. His face is completely still as I disinfect the wound. It should burn, he should wince, I should be there to comfort him as the pain rushes through him, but he is emotionless. I gently rub the back of his hand, soothing whatever pain could be there, or should be.

Jonny is still not talking to me. Radio silence. A familiar uncomfortable silence. Anger rises up in my chest. Not at him, at myself. All too familiar. Panicked. A rush of emotions weighs down on my heart, making it hard to breathe. Heavy. Balling up my hand in my pocket, holding onto nothing, knuckles white. I gently place a bandaid on Jonny's finger, his finger stopped bleeding an hour ago. The air feels colder than the forgotten breakfast. My throat is dry, dehydrated. I couldn’t care less. I’m coming apart, piece by piece. I’ll end up in the same gown by next week.

The house is silent, the air is still. I’m wound up like a spring, and Jonny is staring at the blood dried in the sink. All too heavy, all too futile. And then, a choked ”I’m sorry” from Jonny. An uncomfortable sensation rushes up his spine, causing him to twitch a bit. One that I’m all too familiar with. For the first time, my mind goes blank. What am I meant to do?

“I give up.”
